# Algorithmic Signal–Prior Alignment in Language Model Decision Making

## Abstract

Hybrid systems that pair large language models with classical algorithms can outperform either component alone, but how LLMs process algorithmic signals remains poorly understood. We study this question by presenting eight LLMs with ranked recommendations from information-theoretic (CSS) and decision-theoretic (VOI) algorithms across two sequential reasoning domains — Wordle and Mastermind — under controlled conditions that separate signal-induced behavior change from pre-existing agreement. We find that integration behavior is characterized by three stable regimes: *integration*, where models follow signals at a consistent rate regardless of type; *selective integration*, where models discriminate between signal types based on prior–signal alignment; and *rejection*, where models reference signals in their reasoning but do not change their decisions. These regimes are a stable model-level property, not task-dependent: follow rate correlations across domains exceed r = 0.93, and 7 of 8 models maintain the same regime classification across all domains. A performance-conditioned analysis reveals that signal following is most beneficial for models whose unassisted priors are weakest, with one frontier model gaining 47 percentage points in win rate when following recommendations. We formalize these findings through an alignment-modulated logarithmic opinion pooling model and show that under zero-shot prompting, a constant integration weight suffices for all models — selective integration emerges only under chain-of-thought reasoning, establishing a prompting-modality boundary for signal integration. These results provide empirically grounded design principles for hybrid LLM-algorithm systems: regime classification determines whether signal provision is worthwhile, and architectural integration may be necessary to guarantee signal uptake beyond what prompting alone can achieve.
